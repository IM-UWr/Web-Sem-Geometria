In the modern mathematical language randomness is associated with the
presence of a certain probability measure. If there is a group of
symmetries one can further talk about measures invariant with respect
to this group. However, it turns out that invariance can also be
defined under much weaker assumptions. Various ideas of this theory
have been discovered (and quite often rediscovered) by both
probabilists and geometers. I will give a brief survey describing the
basic notions (holonomy invariant measures on foliations, invariance
with respect to equivalence relations, stochastically homogeneous,
unimodular and stationary graphs or manifolds, invariant random
subgroups) and outline the main problems.
