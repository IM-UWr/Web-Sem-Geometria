A coloring of a graph *G* is nonrepetitive if no simple path of
*G* contains two identical blocks of colors in a row. In 1906 Thue proved
that three colors are sufficient for such coloring of an infinite path.
This result is the starting point of Combinatorics on Words - a wide
discipline with lots of exciting problems, results, and applications
(for instance in a famous Burnside problem for finitely generated groups
of bounded exponent). In the talk I will present some recent
developments in graph theoretic part of this area. Most of them
concentrate around the main conjecture stating that planar graphs are
nonrepetitively colorable with some constant number of colors.
